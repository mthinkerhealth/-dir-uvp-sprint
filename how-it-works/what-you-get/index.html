How it works

The Structure Repeats. The Content Never Does.

Tom Barnes September 22, 2026

A UVP Sprint doesn’t end with a stack of paper. It ends with a decision your leadership team actually made together, in the room, on the record. What gets written down afterward is the documentation of that decision, not the decision itself.

That document isn’t invented fresh each time. It takes the same shape whether the client is a regenerative farm or a senior home-care agency, two businesses that share nothing else. The shape holds because it’s built to hold the same weight every time: a single answer, tested the same way, regardless of what the answer turns out to be.

That shape includes a stated position: who the answer is for, what problem it solves, and why a specific alternative loses to it. It includes a small number of distinct pillars underneath that position, each built from four parts: what’s promised, how it’s delivered, what proves it’s true, and where the claim stops, so nobody in the company later stretches it past what it can carry. It includes a clear description of who the business is built for, and who it isn’t, stated plainly enough that a lower-fit prospect can be told the truth instead of chased. It includes a map of what the customer is choosing between when they don’t choose this business, industry by real alternative, not a vague “the competition.” It includes a register of what the company can say publicly and what it can’t, organized by risk, so marketing and legal aren’t refighting the same argument every quarter. And it includes language the team can actually say out loud, in a real conversation with a real customer, tested down to whether a person would stumble over the sentence.

None of that gets invented as a checklist and filled in; each piece gets extracted, argued over, and tested against the room’s own evidence during the day itself. The shape is identical across industries because the method that produces it is identical. The content inside it is never identical, because it’s built entirely from what’s actually true about the business in the room, not from a set of prompts anyone could answer for anyone.

What you’re actually paying for is that the answer inside it is true, tested by your own leadership team, and something they were all actually asked about, not just the person who talks the most in meetings.

FAQ

Is the deliverable a template I fill in myself?
No. Every piece of it is built from what your leadership team actually says and defends during the day. Two clients in completely different industries produce the same structure and almost nothing else in common.
Do I get the same set of documents every time?
Five parts of the structure show up in every engagement so far: the stated position, the message pillars, the fit description, the alternatives map, and the claims register, plus working language for real conversations. A standing reference document for keeping later communications consistent has only been confirmed in one engagement to date, so it isn’t presented here as a guarantee.
